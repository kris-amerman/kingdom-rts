# Backlog

Planned work, one story per PR. CLAUDE.md says what the game should do and docs/design.md says why; this file tracks what we're building next and what was decided along the way, so details aren't lost between sessions.

## How to use this
- **One story = one atomic PR.** Keep stories small enough to review in one sitting.
- **Status:** `planned` → `in progress` → `in review` → `done`. Done stories move to the Done section with their PR number.
- **Update the story as you go:** when work starts (status, branch), when scope changes (say what moved and why), and when it ships (PR number, anything left over).
- **Decisions** worth remembering go in the story's Notes, and anything that changes the game's rules goes into CLAUDE.md as well.
- **New ideas** that aren't ready to be stories go under Later.

Story template:
```
### S-000 Title
- **Status:** planned | **Branch:** feature/... | **PR:** - | **Depends on:** -
- **Goal:** one sentence: what's true when this is done.
- **Scope:** what's in.
- **Out of scope:** what's deliberately not in.
- **Test:** automated tests added (unit tests, GameTests) + a short manual checklist (client-side visuals only).
- **Notes:** decisions, open questions.
```

### S-048 Lumberjack slow to start in the reach test
- **Status:** in progress | **Branch:** feature/lumberjack-reach-flake | **PR:** - | **Depends on:** S-029
- **Goal:** a lumberjack starts on a nearby tree promptly every time, and `reachesATree10BlocksAway` passes every run.
- **Scope:**
  - Find why a lumberjack sometimes waits about 450 ticks before setting off for a tree 10 blocks away. Seen once in CI on PR #47: felling started after 616 ticks, over the test's 600, against about 165 in every other run. Per the log, it only started walking near the end.
  - Fix the cause: in the lumberjack's code if it's a real stall (a wait or retry it shouldn't hit), or in the test if it's the test's timing.
- **Out of scope:** other lumberjack changes.
- **Test:** the cause shown from the CI log, the chunk wait measured locally (up to 233 ticks), and the GameTests passing 6 runs back to back (scope change: the planned 20 runs back to back and a sabotage check can't tell the fix apart here, as the wait comes from CI's slower disk, which local runs don't reproduce).
- **Notes:**
  - Found on PR #47 (S-026), which doesn't touch the lumberjack. A rerun of the failed job passed.
  - **The cause, the test's clock, not the lumberjack:**
    - The CI log shows the lumberjack never moved from where it was spawned until its walk began, about 600 ticks in. Then it felled straight away, with no step timing out and no "no trees" wait. It wasn't being ticked at all.
    - The test forces the claim's chunks, then starts its clock at once. A chunk only ticks entities once their section has loaded, which happens off the server thread. And the GameTest server runs ticks back to back, without waiting between them (`GameTestServer#waitUntilNextTick` only runs pending tasks). So waiting on the disk shows up as hundreds of ticks on a slow machine, and the lumberjack is idle until its chunk ticks.
    - Locally, over 6 runs, the wait reached 233 ticks, and about half of the ~165 ticks the 10-block test used to report was this wait. With the old clock, a slower disk only had to add a few hundred more.
    - Not a bug in play: a unit in a chunk that isn't ticking doesn't act, as vanilla mobs don't.
  - **The fix:** the reach tests start their clock once the lumberjack's position and the tree's are entity-ticking (`ServerLevel#isPositionEntityTicking`), and log how long that took. The 30-second limit is unchanged; the timeout allows another minute for the wait.
  - **Lesson for other tests:** time limits in GameTests should count from when the units can act, not from when their chunks were forced.

### S-026 Lumberjack hut building
- **Status:** done | **Branch:** feature/lumberjack-hut-building | **PR:** #47 | **Depends on:** S-031, S-034
- **Goal:** a lumberjack hut is a real building, commissioned from a template, built by builders, and managed from its hut block.
- **Scope:**
  - **Core blocks from data** (the general part, reused by later functional buildings): a building's data can name its core block, e.g. `"core": "kingdomrts:lumberjack_hut"`.
    - The core is part of the template, which must contain it exactly once.
    - While the building is built, its construction site block stands on the core's spot (scope change, owner's call: the first plan put the core down at once). When it's finished, the site block becomes the core.
    - Clicking the site block shows the site panel while the building is built, and the core shows its own panel (the hut panel) once built.
    - Plain buildings keep their site block on a clear spot, as before.
  - **The workplace starts employing once its building is built.** Until then it's only a building being built: no hiring, and it isn't offered to job seekers.
  - **The building's stock:** what's left in the site stock when it's finished moves into the core, as the hut's starting stock (e.g. spare axes).
  - **A lumberjack hut template** (small, wooden) and its building definition.
  - **Demolishing** the building (by builders, S-038) takes its hut block down too, so the workplace goes and its workers lose their jobs (existing workplace code).
- **Out of scope:**
  - other huts, the town hall (S-027), homes, repair;
  - a Demolish action and builder assignment on the hut panel (dropped with the owner's go-ahead): the nation interface's Demolish (S-035) already demolishes any of your buildings and puts idle builders on it.
- **Test:**
  - **Unit tests:** a template's core (missing, once, several), and the building definition with and without a core.
  - **GameTests:**
    - commissioning puts the site block on the hut block's spot (turned too), and it isn't a workplace yet;
    - nobody is hired while it's under construction;
    - once finished, the hut block takes the site block's place with its leftovers, and hires;
    - a builder builds it from storage;
    - demolishing it fires its worker;
    - a placed hut block (the stand-in) still works as before.
  - **Manual:** the hut template's look; the panels switching.
- **Notes:**
  - Owner: homes need their own residence core (a later story, with housing).
  - Placed hut blocks, the stand-in until the nation management interface builds huts, keep working. Whether they stay after that is open.
  - **What was built:**
    - **`BuildingType`** has an optional `core`. Commissioning a building with one puts its site block where the template has the core (`BuildingRules#coreOf`), and refuses a template without the core, or with several.
    - **Finishing** (`BuildingOps#completeIfDone`) closes such a site at once: the site block becomes the core as the template has it, the site's stock moves into the core's, and the core registers (a hut becomes the settlement's workplace, so automatic hiring fills it). Nothing is carried off to storage.
    - **The template** (`structure/buildings/lumberjack_hut.nbt`, from `scripts/gen_lumberjack_hut_nbt.py`) is 5x5 and 4 high: a cobblestone floor, plank walls on log corners, a door, two open windows, a slab roof inside a plank rim, a torch, and the hut block in the middle of the floor. It's in the Build catalog as "Lumberjack hut".
  - **Decisions:**
    - **The site block first, then the core (owner's call):** every building has the same kind of site while it's built (one site block, panel and builder code), and a hut block always means a working hut, never a half-built one.
    - **No crafted hut block (owner's call):** commissioning doesn't need a hut item. Buildings are picked from the catalog (and later paid for in gold), as Direction says, and requiring a crafted hut block is the most recognisable MineColonies mechanic.
    - **The core isn't a material:** it comes with the building, so builders taking it down get nothing back for it (no hut item carried to storage).
    - **No higher than 4:** builders build from the ground, with no scaffolding yet. A first 5-high design left its roof out of their reach from outside, with the ceiling in the way from inside. Templates have to stay within reach until builders can climb.
  - **Tests:**
    - JUnit: `BuildingRulesTest` (a core exactly once, none, several) and `BlueprintTest` (a building's `core` decodes, and is optional).
    - GameTests (`LumberjackHutBuildingGameTests`):
      - the site stands on the hut block's spot, turned as well, and it isn't a workplace while built;
      - finished, the hut block takes its place with the site's leftovers (an axe and planks) and a settler is hired, with nothing dropped;
      - breaking its site cancels it;
      - a builder builds it from storage, and no hut item turns up;
      - builders demolish it: its stock goes to storage, its lumberjack loses the job, and no hut item comes back.
    - Sabotage checks, each caught:
      - the core not registering when finished;
      - the site on a clear spot instead of the core's;
      - a hut item given back for the core.
  - **Stress:** not run. Nothing per-unit was added: finishing and taking down the core are one-off steps.

### S-036 Nation interface: borders view
- **Status:** done | **Branch:** feature/borders-view | **PR:** #46 | **Depends on:** S-032
- **Goal:** a player can toggle a view of settlement borders around them.
- **Scope:**
  - A toggle that draws claim borders near the player, coloured per faction.
  - The server sends nearby claims when the view is switched on, and when claims change.
- **Out of scope:** claim management, faction colours and emblems beyond a simple colour.
- **Test:** a GameTest for what's sent (nearby claims, owners). Manual: the drawn borders.
- **Notes:**
  - **What was built:**
    - **Show borders** on the nation screen is vanilla's checkbox, on its own row above Build and Demolish (owner's call, after a green label and then a lamp on the button).
      - The screen draws its label: vanilla's is light with a shadow, unreadable on the light panel. Clicking the label toggles too.
      - The screen stays open, and the checkbox gives up the focus after a click so it doesn't stay highlighted.
    - **On the ground:** every claim within 12 chunks is sent, and drawn up to 128 blocks away (or the render distance, if shorter), fading out over the last 32. Each is outlined just inside its edge, following the ground, in its owner's colour, with a low see-through wall above the line that fades out upwards.
    - **Underground (owner's call):** a faint curtain runs from the line down to the bottom of the world. It's depth-tested, so it shows wherever a cave, ravine or tunnel crosses the border, and rock hides it as it hides blocks. It's one more quad per block of border in the same meshes.
    - **Key:** a list in the top-left corner names the settlements shown, nearest first, each in its colour and marked "yours", the owner's name, or "unclaimed". It's hidden while F3 is up. With nothing around, it's just the "Borders" title (a "no settlements nearby" note flashed up while the first borders were on their way, which the owner found distracting).
  - **Decisions:**
    - **Borders are public:** anyone sees every claim around them, with or without a faction. The server doesn't need a faction to answer.
    - **Colours** are worked out from the faction's id (`FactionColors`), so they're stable, survive renames, and need nothing saved. Choosing colours is for later.
    - **When borders are sent** (`BorderViewers`):
      - when the view is switched on;
      - again only when claims change (`SettlementData#claimsVersion` goes up when a settlement is founded, dissolved, changes owner or is renamed), or the viewer moves 4 chunks on or changes dimension.
      - Viewers are checked once a second, and a player is sent borders at most every 10 ticks, even switching the view off and on.
      - Renaming a faction doesn't resend: the key shows the old name until the next resend.
    - **Whole claims:** every settlement with a chunk in range is sent whole, so the edge of the range never draws a false border. Caps: 32 settlements, 1024 chunks.
    - **Where claims touch,** each border is drawn 0.1 blocks inside its own claim, so both colours show side by side.
    - **Cost:**
      - Borders are laid on the ground when they arrive, one baked mesh per settlement. Each frame draws only the meshes of settlements within the draw distance.
      - Each tick rechecks the ground under 256 border columns, going round the settlements near enough to draw. A block placed or broken on a border shows within a tick or two (first version: a full pass every 2 seconds, which the owner found slow).
      - A settlement's mesh is baked again only when the ground under one of its columns changed. Stretches in chunks that aren't loaded are left out until they load.
      - `/kingdomrts perf` reports the borders view's drawing time per frame and ground checks per tick (`PerfReport.Borders`).
      - The owner's first live report (one settlement, 384 pieces drawn on average, before the curtain): 0.052 ms a frame on average (0.10 at the 95th percentile), and 0.084 ms a tick checking the ground (0.67 ms worst, the full pass when borders arrive).
      - Their second, with the curtain and an adjacent rival (373 pieces): 0.049 ms a frame (0.102 at the 95th percentile, 0.41 worst), and 0.090 ms a tick checking the ground (1.07 ms worst, again the full pass on arrival).
  - **Tests:**
    - JUnit:
      - `BorderLinesTest`: one chunk's four sides, no border inside a claim, the inset and corners (outside and inside), pieces following the ground;
      - `FactionColorsTest`: stable, grey with no owner, mostly different and always bright;
      - `PayloadSizeTest`: the most borders sent fit 16 KB.
    - GameTests (`NationBordersGameTests`):
      - a member, an outsider and a factionless player are all sent the whole claim in its owner's colour, only the member's marked as their own, and nothing out of range;
      - a viewer is sent borders on switching on, not again while nothing changes, again when the settlement changes hands, and not once switched off;
      - switching off and on again at once waits for the next check, and moving 4 chunks on resends.
    - Sabotage checks, each caught: an owner change not counted as a claims change, moving ignored, no range limit.
    - A bug the tests found: a viewer's "never sent" time started at `Long.MIN_VALUE`, so "now minus last sent" overflowed and the first send never happened.
    - A bug found in-game: borders were laid on the height map that leaves out leaves, which the server never sends to clients, so every border sat at the bottom of the world. They now use `MOTION_BLOCKING` (sent to clients) and step down through leaves; `BorderLinesTest` checks the height map is one clients have.
  - **Stress:** borders added to the request benchmark: 16 µs per call (budget 1 ms). The unit budget still holds (docs/perf.md results).

### S-035 Nation interface: demolish
- **Status:** done | **Branch:** feature/interface-demolish | **PR:** #45 | **Depends on:** S-032, S-038
- **Goal:** a player can demolish their own buildings from the interface.
- **Scope:**
  - **Demolish** shows your settlement's buildings as outlines.
  - Pick one by looking at it, confirm, and the server runs the demolition operation from S-038, so builders take it apart.
  - Other factions' buildings can't be picked or demolished.
- **Out of scope:** gold costs.
- **Test:** GameTests for the request (own building demolished, another faction's refused). Manual: outlines and picking.
- **Notes:**
  - **What was built:**
    - **Demolish** on the nation screen asks the server for the faction's buildings within 96 blocks (finished, being built or coming down; up to 64, nearest first) and outlines them all.
    - **Picking:** the one the player looks at (up to 48 blocks) is highlighted in white. Ones already coming down are orange.
    - **Stopping a demolition (owner's ask):** the same two right-clicks on an orange building stop its demolition (outline yellow, "Right-click again to stop demolishing the storage shed"), answered with "Stopped demolishing the storage shed". What's been taken down stays down; a site keeps its builders, and a finished building's go back to the pool.
    - **Right-click twice:** the first right-click picks the building (outline red, "Right-click again to demolish the storage shed"). The second, within 3 seconds and still looking at it, orders the demolition.
    - **Leaving:** Esc or Backspace leaves, and so does a successful order. Demolish and the building preview end each other.

A finished building has no site block, so otherwise only a command could assign them. `BuilderOps.assignIdle` is a faction operation of its own, for AI factions too.
    - **Same rules as the command:** the request checks the settlement with `NationRequests.settlementProblem` (its first user), then runs `BuildingOps.orderDemolition`, the same operation as `/kingdomrts building demolish`.

    - **Stopping checks the owner:** `BuildingOps.stopDemolition` now takes the acting faction and refuses another faction's building, like `orderDemolition`, so AI factions can't stop each other's demolitions. `/kingdomrts building cancel` stays an admin command and acts for the building's owner.

    - **Cost:** outlines are a few lines per building each frame, so there's nothing to bake. The building list is rate limited like the sites, and refreshed every 5 seconds while picking.
  - **Tests:**
    - JUnit (`BuildingPickerTest`): the nearest box in the line of sight, nothing out of range or behind, and standing inside one picks it.
    - GameTests (`NationDemolishGameTests`):
      - a member is shown both sheds (finished and being built) with their boxes, but not twice in a row, nothing without a faction, and nothing from far away;
      - demolishing your own shed orders it and puts two of three idle builders on it, and says so;
      - refusals: another faction's member, no faction, no building there, already being demolished;
      - stopping your own demolition leaves an ordinary built shed and frees its builder, and says so; refused for another faction (through the operation too), no faction, and a building that isn't being demolished.
    - Sabotage checks, each caught:
      - acting for the building's owner instead of the player;
      - no ownership check when stopping a demolition;
      - idle builders not assigned;
      - no rate limit.

### S-047 Flaky demolition tests
- **Status:** done | **Branch:** feature/demolish-flakes | **PR:** #44 | **Depends on:** S-038
- **Goal:** S-038's demolition GameTests pass every run.
- **Scope:**
  - Find why `withNoRoomInStorageItHoldsOntoItsLoad` and `everyMaterialGoesBackToStorage` sometimes time out "waiting for the shed to be gone" (seen in about 1 in 8 runs during S-034 and S-022, and during S-038 itself).
  - Fix the cause: in the builders' code if it's a real bug (a builder that can stall), or in the tests if it's their timing.
- **Out of scope:** other changes to demolition.
- **Test:** the fixed tests pass in 20 runs back to back. A sabotage check reproduces the old failure if the cause is put back.
- **Notes:**
  - Found while working on S-034 and S-022. Two tests failing with the same symptom suggested one cause, and it was.
  - **The cause, a real bug in unit movement:**
    - When a walk gets stuck, its last resort is a hop: `Footing.escapeSpot` moves the unit to a standable spot within 8 blocks that isn't closer to its target. Nothing checked what was in between.
    - In the tests, a builder stuck at the platform's north edge, heading for storage to the south-east, found that every spot on its own platform that wasn't closer was off the edge. It hopped onto the next test's platform, across a gap, held its load "with no room in storage", and walked at storage forever.
    - In a real world, the same could strand a unit across a ravine or on a neighbouring roof.
  - **How it was found:**
    - The demolition tests' timeout message now says which of the shed's blocks are left, and where each builder is, what it's doing (new `Unit.jobActivity`, the current job step) and what it carries.
    - 40 temporary copies of the two tests in one run reproduced the stall about once in 50. The positions showed the builder outside the platform, walking at storage that had room.
  - **The fix** (`Footing.escapeSpot`): a hop must never end somewhere the unit couldn't walk back from.
    - **No gaps:** every column it passes over has ground at most a block below its lower end (`crossesGap`), since a unit can climb back up one block.
    - **No ledges:** it doesn't end more than a block below both where the unit stands and its target. Climbing down from a tree to a target on the ground stays fine, because the target is low.
    - Climbing out of pits and enclosures still works.
  - **Tests:**
    - New `FootingGameTests`: a unit on an island never hops across a gap or down off the ledge, and a unit boxed in by walls still hops out.
    - Sabotage checks, each caught:
      - allowing hops across gaps (the island test fails, and a demolition copy stalled again in the same run);
      - allowing hops down off ledges (the island test fails).
    - **Confidence:** with the fix, 4 runs with 40 demolition copies each (160 demolitions) and 3 normal runs all passed. Before it, the copies stalled within a few runs. That's well past the 20 runs the story asked for.
    - **Stress:** within budget. The lumberjacks and builders still worked, which the stress test checks, so units still get unstuck.
  - Side finding: in this NeoForge version `GameTestHelper.relativePos` gives rotated coordinates, so the diagnostics subtract the test's corner instead.

### S-046 Bake the preview into a mesh
- **Status:** done | **Branch:** feature/preview-mesh | **PR:** #43 | **Depends on:** S-022
- **Goal:** drawing a building preview and the unfinished sites around it stays under its 1 ms per frame budget, whatever their size.
- **Scope:**
  - **The ghost:** build its blocks into a cached mesh (a vertex buffer) once per building and rotation, and draw that each frame, moved to where the ghost is. Moving it costs nothing, and turning it rebuilds the mesh.
  - **The unfinished sites:** each site's mesh is built when it arrives, and rebuilt when its blocks still to show change (builders place one) or the light around it changes enough to matter. Not every frame.
  - **The tint** (red where it can't go) and see-through alpha are applied when drawing the mesh, not baked in, so a change of answer doesn't rebuild it.
  - Keep what already works: hidden touching faces, see-through chests, drawing pulled toward the camera, solid sites lit like the world.
- **Out of scope:** changing what's shown.
- **Test:**
  - JUnit for when meshes rebuild (pure rules: rotation, site blocks changed).
  - Measurement: a `/kingdomrts perf` report with a preview up among 30 or so sheds is under 1 ms per frame, and well under the first report's 1.45 ms with 167 blocks.
  - Manual: everything looks as before.
- **Notes:**
  - from the owner's first `/kingdomrts perf` report (S-022), where the preview drew 167 blocks at 1.45 ms per frame, about 9 µs a block, mostly the ambient-occlusion lighting maths done afresh every frame. Mesh baking was already the named next lever in docs/perf.md.
  - **What was built:**
    - `client/BakedMesh`: blocks drawn once into vertex buffers on the graphics card, one per render type, then drawn from there each frame (with a tint and a depth-write choice). Closing it frees them.
    - **The ghost** is baked once per building and turn, against its stand-in level, with its alpha baked in. The red tint goes through the shader's colour when it's drawn.
    - **Each unfinished site** is baked with the world's light, in each block's moving-block render types, relative to its lowest corner, and rebuilt when the blocks it still shows change. A refresh every 5 seconds keeps sites that are already shown, mesh and all.
    - Chests and other entity-style blocks are still drawn each frame (few, and on their own textures).
  - **Decisions:**
    - **The ghost is drawn without writing depth.** A baked mesh can't be sorted back to front each frame, as the per-frame drawing was, so faces drawn first would hide the ones behind them. The ghost's far walls still show through its near ones.
    - **Day and night still change**, because the light is baked as light levels and the lightmap turns them into brightness at draw time. A site's mesh is rebuilt for block changes, not for time.
    - **Meshes are freed** when the preview ends, when sites stop being shown, on leaving the world, and on a resource reload (F3+T, resource packs), since textures may move around the atlas. Everything is rebaked on the next frame.
    - `util/Rebuild` remembers what a mesh was built for (the turned blocks, the blocks still shown), so it's rebuilt only when that changes.
  - **Tests:**
    - JUnit (`RebuildTest`): a preview is rebuilt when it turns or changes building, not when it moves, and a site when its shown blocks change.
    - Sabotage check: a rebuild rule that never rebuilds (`RebuildTest` fails).
    - The GameTests and build pass. Nothing server-side changed.
    - **The owner's report** (2026-10-22, 80 s): the building preview took **0.22 ms per frame on average** (0.37 ms 95th percentile) for 185 blocks, against 1.45 ms (2.82 ms) for 167 before. That's about 6½ times faster, and well under the 1 ms budget. The worst frame (23 ms) is most likely a rebuild. Everything looked as before.
  - **Fixed along the way (from S-033):** the log said `Sheets loaded too early, modded registry-based materials may not work correctly`, on every launch since S-033. `BuildPreviewClient` held render types from vanilla's `Sheets` in static fields, and NeoForge loads the class at start-up to find its event handlers, before other mods register their wood types (their signs and chests could get the wrong textures). They're now in a nested class that Java loads only when a preview is first drawn.

### S-025 Builders place blocks

### S-010 Building templates and sites
- **Status:** done | **Branch:** feature/building-sites | **PR:** #24 | **Depends on:** S-001
- **Goal:** a settlement can have a building commissioned at a site from a template.
- **Scope:**
  - **Templates:**
    - vanilla structure files at `data/<ns>/structure/<path>.nbt`, loaded through the level's structure manager;
    - a definition per building in the `kingdomrts:building` datapack registry (`data/<ns>/kingdomrts/building/<name>.json`: `structure` and `name`);
    - the sample is `kingdomrts:storage_shed`.
  - **Commission** (`BuildingOps.commission`, a faction operation): records a site (type, template corner, rotation, footprint) on the settlement. It's refused if:
    - it isn't in a settlement's claim, or it's another faction's claim;
    - it doesn't fit inside one claim, or not within build height;
    - it overlaps another building, or covers the town hall, a workplace or a storage block.
  - **Remaining work** (`BuildingOps.remaining`): blocks missing, blocks in the way, and the materials needed. It never loads chunks.
  - **Commands:** `/kingdomrts building commission <type> <pos> [<rotation>] [faction <name>]`, `list`, `remaining <pos>`, `cancel <pos>`, and `finish <pos>` (a cheat that builds the site instantly, for testing).
- **Out of scope:** the build tool UI and ghost preview (Direction), gold, damage repair, builders (S-011).
- **Test:**
  - **Unit tests:**
    - `BuildingRulesTest`: placing and turning, footprints, matching, remaining work, costs, unplaceable blocks;
    - `BlueprintTest`: reading templates, rejecting broken or huge ones, the building definition;
    - `BuildingTest`: a site survives saving.
  - **GameTests (`BuildingGameTests`, 8):**
    - commissioning, and refusals: outside any claim, someone else's, partly outside, over the hall, overlapping;
    - remaining work on a fresh site and after some blocks are placed by hand;
    - `finish` (with an obstruction cleared), a rotated building's door facing the new way, `cancel`, `list`.
  - **Manual:** the shed's look and its rotations, via `finish`.
- **Notes:**
  - **A block matches** if it's the same block facing the same way: facing, axis, half, hinge, attach face, rotation, slab type, rail shape and bed part must match (owner's call). Other properties are ignored (waterlogged, open, powered, leaf distance, stair shape, chest pairing), so opening a door doesn't make a building unfinished or, later, damaged.
  - **Rotation:** a building turns about its footprint's middle, so a square one stays over the same ground. `<pos>` is the template's corner before turning.
  - **Air in a template means "must be clear":** a solid block there is an obstruction, and so is grass or snow. Structure voids mean "don't care".
  - **Costs:** a door, bed or tall plant costs one item (on its lower half or foot), a double slab costs two, and a wall torch costs a torch. Blocks with no item (fire, fluids) are listed as unplaceable.
  - **Reading templates:** vanilla keeps a template's blocks private, so `Blueprint.read` parses the template's saved NBT (the public structure-file format). No access transformer is needed.
    - Blueprints are cached per structure and cleared on datapack reload.
    - Templates over 32k blocks are rejected.
    - All template access goes through `BuildingTemplates`, so Structurize (design.md) could replace it later.
  - **`finish`** sets blocks without shape updates while placing (so a door's lower half doesn't pop off before its upper half is there), then notifies neighbours. Block entity contents (a chest's items) aren't placed.
  - **The sample shed** was generated with a small script. To re-author it in-game:
    1. Build it.
    2. Save it with a structure block named `kingdomrts:buildings/storage_shed`.
    3. Copy the file from `run/world/generated/kingdomrts/structure/` into `src/main/resources/data/kingdomrts/structure/buildings/`.
  - **Test fix:** the local GameTest world (`run/world`) keeps factions between runs, so a command that looked a test faction up by name could find a leftover one with the same name. `TestSupport.faction` names now end with a per-run suffix. CI starts fresh, so it never showed up there.
  - **No stress run needed:** nothing per unit or per tick was added.
  - **Follow-ups:**
    - a workplace block in a template becomes a workplace when built;
    - an outline or preview of a site;
    - damage detection (built buildings compared with their template);
    - evaluating Structurize for the build tool.

### S-014 Lumberjack works from its hut
- **Status:** done | **Branch:** feature/lumberjack-hut | **PR:** #20 | **Depends on:** S-017
- **Goal:** a lumberjack works around its hut, brings its logs back to the hut's stock, and takes its axes from there.
- **Scope:**
  - **Work area:** a radius around the hut, intersected with the claim. It is set in the hut panel and capped by a maximum in data.
  - It picks up tree drops (logs, saplings, sticks, apples), not only logs, and gathers the drops around a felled tree.
  - It deposits what it collected into the hut's stock after each tree or when full, and takes axes from the stock.
  - Without room in the stock it keeps its logs and waits.
- **Out of scope:** replanting and wood filters (S-016), showing the work area, buying tools.
- **Test:** GameTests: trees outside the work area are ignored; logs and other drops end up in the hut's stock; it takes an axe from the stock; a full stock makes it wait; it clears a felled tree's canopy but leaves neighbouring canopies standing; it fells several trees before going back. Manual: the work-area setting in the panel. Checked by the owner, with the full loop.
- **Notes:**
  - Replaces the earlier "Lumberjack hauls logs to storage": logs now go to the hut, not settlement storage.
  - **The loop** (revised after owner feedback):
    1. Find a tree in the work area; walk beside it; fell it.
    2. Clear its canopy (below).
    3. Collect anything wanted still lying within 8 blocks, for up to 5 s.
    4. Repeat. It goes back to the hut only when it should deposit (pure rule `LumberjackJob.shouldDeposit`: fewer than 2 empty slots, or no room for logs), when there are no trees left and it's carrying something, or when it has no axe and the hut's stock has one. Walking back after every tree wasted most of its time.
    5. At the hut: deposit everything except axes (`Stock.insert` tops up stacks, then fills empty slots), and take the stock's best axe if it has none.
    6. If the stock was full and it still has no room for logs, it waits and tries again.
  - **Clearing the canopy (owner feedback: most drops were missed, because leaves take minutes to decay):**
    - **How vanilla works:** every natural leaf stores its distance to the nearest log. After a log is removed, distances update over a few ticks, and a leaf with no log within 6 (distance 7) decays, but only on a random tick, which is the slow part.
    - **What the lumberjack does** (`ClearCanopyStep`): waits 10 ticks for the distances to settle, then decays every such leaf around the tree itself. Drops come from the leaf's own loot table, so saplings, sticks and apples arrive at vanilla's chances, straight into its inventory. It clears 4 per tick, so the canopy falls away over a second or two. The pure check is `isOrphanedLeaf`.
    - **Dense forests:** leaves still within 6 of another tree's log keep a lower distance and stay, so canopies that touch are handled by vanilla's own rule. No search through the connected forest is needed.
    - **Bounded work:** it scans only the felled tree's bounds plus vanilla's 6-block leaf range (`TreeRules.canopyBox`), 512 blocks per tick, once per tree.
  - **Work area:**
    - `Workplace.workRadius` is saved per workplace. Its starting value and maximum come from the workplace data (`work_radius` 16 and `max_work_radius` 48 for the lumberjack hut), and data with a starting radius above the maximum is rejected.
    - The hut panel has a "Work area" row with −/+ buttons, in steps of 4 down to a minimum of 4. Being on the workplace, it's ready for every job.
    - The search keeps only claim chunks that reach into the area (`WorkArea.touchesChunk`), then only columns within the radius (`WorkArea.contains`), measured across the ground.
  - **Drops:** lumberjacks pick up logs, saplings, sticks and apples, and nothing else. The saplings are kept for replanting (S-016).
  - **Hut panel layout (owner feedback: the panel ran off the screen):** it's now two pages of one fixed, vanilla-sized panel, like MineColonies' hut windows.
    - **Overview:** details, the work area with −/+, and buttons for automatic hiring, "Workers (n/m)…" and Stock.
    - **Workers:** one paged list (6 rows a page) of "Working here" with Fire and "Looking for work" with Hire, then Back and paging.
    - The current page (`WorkplaceInfo.view`) is sent by the server, so hiring, firing and the live refresh keep the player on the workers page.
    - Settlers looking for work are sent in full (up to 64) and paged by the shared `WorkplaceInfo.rows()`.
    - Text isn't scaled down: Minecraft's pixel font blurs when shrunk.
  - **Profiler sections** (`kingdomrts:job`, `kingdomrts:job_seeking`, `kingdomrts:capture`) show our share of the tick in Minecraft's profiler (`/debug start`/`/debug stop`) and tools like Spark.
  - Existing lumberjack GameTests now count logs carried plus logs in the hut's stock. "Doesn't chop with no room" became "waits when it and its hut are full".
  - **Flakes:** GameTests run 3 times in a row without flakes.
  - **Sabotage checks:** no depositing; logs-only pickup; not taking the hut's axe; no work-area filtering; skipping the canopy step; clearing leaves whatever their distance (the shared-canopy test); depositing after every tree. Each fails the matching tests.
    - The work-area GameTest can only reliably catch both filters disabled together. Whether the far tree's chunk touches the area depends on where the test sits on the chunk grid. The column filter on its own is covered by `WorkAreaTest`.

### S-009 Lumberjack fells trees
- **Status:** done | **Branch:** feature/lumberjack | **PR:** #15 | **Depends on:** S-007, S-008
- **Goal:** a lumberjack unit fells natural trees in its settlement's claim and keeps the logs, without supervision.
- **Scope:**

  - **Pickup:** lumberjacks only pick up logs.
- **Out of scope:** workplaces (S-015), work areas and depositing (S-014), replanting and wood filters (S-016), taking tools from stock, payment and gold.
- **Test:**
  - Unit tests: the step runner (order, time limits, stopping), tree detection, break times.
  - GameTests:
    - fells a tree and keeps its logs;
    - leaves player-built logs alone;
    - recovers when its tree disappears on the way;
    - ignores trees outside its claim;
    - doesn't chop with no room;
    - idles without a settlement;
    - break time matches vanilla's for a player;
    - felling by hand takes a player's time;
    - uses and wears its axe;
    - carries on by hand when the axe breaks;
    - reaches a tree open on one side only;
    - gets down from a tree top;
    - climbs out of a pit;
    - skips a tree it can't reach.
  - Manual: watch one work; crack animation, axe in hand, behaviour in a real forest. Checked by the owner.
- **Notes:**
  - **Split:** the original S-009 (fell trees and haul logs to storage) was split, to keep PRs small. After the owner's feedback, the follow-ups became workplaces (S-015), working from the hut (S-014) and hut settings (S-016).
  - **Job framework (`unit/job`):** a `Job` hands out `JobStep`s. `JobRunner` ticks the current step, fails it when it runs past its time limit, and asks the job for the next step (passing how the last one went). `JobGoal` runs it as an AI goal. Shared steps: `WalkToStep`, `WaitStep`.
  - **Search:** a heightmap lookup per claim column (the highest non-leaf block), 128 columns per tick, skipping unloaded chunks. Candidate logs are then checked nearest first with `TreeRules`, 4 per tick. A 3×3 claim takes about 20 ticks.
  - **Tree rule (pure, `TreeRules`):** logs connected in all 26 directions (so branches count), at most 256 of them, and at least 3 natural leaves touching. Leaves placed by players are persistent, so they never count.
  - **Breaking (owner feedback):** breaking was instant at first.
    - **Timing:** `BreakTime` copies vanilla's player formula (tool speed, Efficiency via the `MINING_EFFICIENCY` attribute, `BLOCK_BREAK_SPEED`, the ÷30 or ÷100 harvest rule). Units now have the two mining attributes, so enchanted tools work. Oak takes 60 ticks by hand, 30 with a wooden axe, 10 with an iron one. A GameTest checks it against vanilla's own numbers for a player. `chop_ticks` was removed from the data.
    - **Felling:** highest log first, with vanilla's crack animation and arm swings.
    - **Axes:** while chopping it holds its fastest axe from its inventory, which takes durability per log. It switches to the next axe or bare hands when one breaks, and puts the axe back in its slot afterwards. The held axe always drops if the unit dies.
    - **Drops:** they go straight into the inventory, so logs can't get stuck in the canopy.
  - **Pathfinding (owner feedback: it skipped trees, got stuck, and once got stranded on a tree):**
    - **Stand spot:** it walks to a standable spot within 2 blocks of the trunk (solid ground that isn't part of a tree, room for its body), preferring one it has a full path to (checking at most 8). Aiming at the log itself is what led it up trees.
    - **Exact spot:** it paths to the spot itself (accuracy 0). Vanilla's `moveTo(x, y, z)` happily stops a block short, which left units just outside reach until the stuck handling nudged them, so walks took about 15 s instead of 4.
    - **Recovery ladder** (in `WalkToStep`):
      1. re-plan once a second;
      2. no progress for 3 s, or standing on a tree: step to a random nearby spot (twice);
      3. still stuck: hop to a safe spot within 8 blocks (the owner chose short teleports as the last resort). It never moves closer to the target, so it can't hop over a wall to a tree it couldn't walk to.
      4. still stuck: fail, and the job skips that tree for a minute.
    - **Restriction:** workers no longer keep within 6 blocks of their post, since that's for soldiers and idle units.
  - **Recovery:**
    - a tree it can't reach is skipped for a minute;
    - a tree that disappears on the way is replaced by the next one;
    - with no settlement, no room or no trees, it waits and checks again.
  - `Occupation.wantsToPickUp` lets each occupation filter what it picks up (lumberjack: logs only).
  - **Flakes:** GameTests run 3 times in a row without flakes.
  - **Sabotage checks** that fail the matching tests:
    - logs without leaves count as trees;
    - chopping with no room;
    - instant breaking;
    - ignoring the axe;
    - no teleport fallback (both the pit and tree-top tests fail);
    - walking at the log instead of a stand spot (the pit test fails).

- **Notes:**
  - GameTest coordinates: the framework puts a structure block at y = 0, so the template's floor is at y = 1 and things stand at y = 2 (`TestSupport.FLOOR_Y`).
